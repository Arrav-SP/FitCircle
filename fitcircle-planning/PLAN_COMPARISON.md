## 1. Summary verdict

The second blueprint is technically sound in its principles: PostgreSQL as source of truth, row level security everywhere, a streak ledger separated from streak state, idempotent evaluation. Its stack choice survives verification.

Its weakness is scope and sequencing. It plans a full tracker, a feed with comments, streaks, chat and two health integrations in eight weeks, with no statement of who is building it. For one person at 10–15 hours a week that is roughly 100 hours of work budgeted against several hundred hours of features. It also assumes iOS and Android native apps from the start, which the owner cannot test or pay for yet.

The independent plan keeps the second blueprint's engineering principles and cuts the product to what makes it different.

## 3. Material disagreements

### 3.1 Scope of the first release

- **Difference.** The blueprint's P0 has meals, a feed and comments. The independent plan defers all three.
- **Consequence.** Each deferred item removes tables, screens, policies and, for comments, a moderation obligation. My estimate is that the cut removes about a third of the pre-launch work.
- **Verdict.** Independent plan. The owner chose streak-first during brainstorming.
- **What the blueprint gets right.** "Social by choice" and "privacy before virality" are the right principles and are kept.

### 3.2 Platforms

- **Difference.** The blueprint assumes both native apps. The independent plan ships Android native and a web app.
- **Consequence.** Native iOS needs the US$99 yearly programme [S21] and regular device access. The web app costs nothing extra to distribute and is testable in any browser. The cost is push: Expo's notification library does not support web [S1], and Apple allows web push only for sites added to the home screen [S20].
- **Verdict.** A third option the owner proposed: Android plus web. Native iOS follows when usage justifies it.
- **Evidence.** [S1], [S20], [S21].

### 3.3 Where trusted logic runs

- **Difference.** The blueprint puts trusted operations in `supabase/functions/` (edge functions, TypeScript). The independent plan puts them in PostgreSQL functions.
- **Consequence.** An edge function evaluating a streak makes several database calls and must build its own locking. A database function does it in one transaction with a row lock. Database functions also need care: a `security definer` function bypasses row level security and must sit outside the exposed schema with a fixed `search_path` [S7].
- **Verdict.** Independent plan, with the hardening rules written into the blueprint.

### 3.4 Chat and realtime

- **Difference.** The blueprint has realtime chat in P1 and asks whether it should be P0.
- **Consequence.** Chat is free text between two users. It needs reporting, blocking and timely action on reports [S17]. Supabase's change feed applies row level security to inserts and updates but not to delete events [S8], so a naive chat can leak the fact and identifiers of deleted messages.
- **Verdict.** Neither P0 nor P1. Chat comes after comments, after launch. Pairs already have a messaging app; the streak does not need ours.

### 3.5 Health integration timing

- **Difference.** The blueprint gives HealthKit and Health Connect one week (week 7).
- **Consequence.** Health Connect needs a development build, a config plugin, API 26+ [S19], an extra background-read permission [S18], and two Play declarations with written justification [S16]. HealthKit needs the native iOS app the owner cannot yet build or test.
- **Verdict.** Independent plan: no imports at launch. The data model records `source` and `external_id` from day one, which is the blueprint's own good idea applied early.

### 3.6 Daily streak versus scheduled days

- **Difference.** The blueprint (and the vision) say both partners qualify every day.
- **Consequence.** A daily workout requirement with no rest days pushes users toward overtraining or toward logging fake workouts to protect the streak. Either outcome damages the product.
- **Verdict.** Independent plan: agreed weekdays. This departs from the vision's wording and is logged as a proposed decision for the owner to confirm.

### 3.7 Late entries and historical edits

- **Difference.** The blueprint asks for "defined finalization deadlines" and "recalculation when relevant records are edited or deleted" without fixing either.
- **Consequence.** Unlimited recalculation means a streak can change weeks later, and a user can restore a lost streak by backdating a workout.
- **Verdict.** A fixed finalisation window (three hours after the day ends). Before it, recalculate freely. After it, the day is permanent.

### 3.8 Schema size

- **Difference.** The blueprint lists 27 tables. The independent plan has 17.
- **Consequence.** Every table needs policies and tests.
- **Verdict by table group:**

| Blueprint tables | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `profiles`, `user_settings` | Keep as `profiles` and `user_private` | The split is needed for row-level access, and the name says why |
| `friend_requests`, `friendships`, `user_blocks` | Keep; add `reports` | Reports are a store requirement [S17] |
| `workout_sessions`, `workout_exercises`, `workout_sets` | Keep; add `exercises` catalogue | The blueprint omitted the catalogue |
| `food_entries` | Defer | Nutrition is post-launch |
| `water_entries`, `sleep_sessions`, `daily_activity_summaries` | Replace with `metric_entries` | One table with a type column covers steps, water and sleep, with provenance |
| `activity_posts`, `post_reactions` | Keep as `shared_events`, `event_reactions` | System-generated events only |
| `post_comments`, `conversations`, `conversation_members`, `messages` | Defer | Comments and chat are post-launch |
| `streak_challenges`, `streak_daily_progress`, `streak_state` | Keep as `streak_challenges` and `streak_days` | Streak state is two columns on the challenge, recomputed from the ledger |
| `streak_participants` | Drop for now | Exactly two participants fit as two columns; a participants table returns with group challenges |
| `notifications`, `push_tokens` | Keep as `push_tokens`, `notification_log` | The log is for idempotent sends, not an in-app inbox |
| `health_connections`, `health_sync_cursors`, `health_imported_records` | Defer | Created with the import feature |

### 3.9 Timeline

- **Difference.** Eight weeks versus 25–32 weeks.
- **Consequence.** The blueprint states no hours. At 10–15 hours a week, eight weeks is 80–120 hours, less than my estimate for the streak engine and friends system alone.
- **Verdict.** Independent plan, stated in hours so the owner can re-scale.

## 4. Strong ideas unique to each

**Second blueprint**

- Six product principles, especially "consistency over perfection" and "privacy before virality".
- Separating challenge configuration, daily progress and aggregate state.
- A platform-independent health provider interface (kept for when imports arrive; not built before).
- A nutrition provider abstraction (same treatment).
- Explicit note that frontend visibility controls do not replace backend authorization.

**Independent plan**

- Scheduled workout days.
- Finalisation window with permanent days.
- Android plus web as the launch platforms.
- No free text between users at launch.
- No realtime at launch.
- Logic in PostgreSQL functions with database tests.
- A roadmap in hours that includes the mandatory closed test.

## 5. Weaknesses shared by both

- **Self-reported workouts.** Neither plan can verify a manual workout. Stated openly; imported-step streaks later reduce it.
- **Growth depends on pairs.** A user whose friend does not join has little reason to stay. Neither plan has a solo fallback beyond the tracker.
- **Moderation is one person.** Reports go to the owner. Fine for dozens of users; needs a plan at thousands.
- **Offline is shallow.** Both rely on cached reads and retried writes. A workout logged in a gym with no signal must not be lost; this needs explicit testing.

## 6. Requirements missing from both (added in the final blueprint)

- Age confirmation (18+) at sign-up and its legal reason [S22].
- A privacy policy and a consent notice in plain language.
- Web-reachable account deletion, required by Google Play in addition to in-app deletion [S15].
- The Play health-apps declaration, which applies to health apps generally [S16].
- The twelve-tester, fourteen-day closed test for new personal accounts [S13].
- Limits: challenges per user, requests per day, reports per day.
- What happens to a challenge when a partner deletes their account, unfriends or blocks.
- Invitation expiry.
- An exercise catalogue and how custom exercises are scoped.
- Body weight as an optional input for calorie estimates, and what is shown without it.
- Database backup and restore, and that free-tier projects pause when idle [S6].
- A way for the owner to act on reports (documented SQL procedures at launch, not an admin app).
